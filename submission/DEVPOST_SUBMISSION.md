## Project title

**FloodGuard AI — Turning Flood Signals into Community Health Preparedness**

## One-line pitch

A bilingual flood-health preparedness prototype that connects weather context, transparent risk indicators, community reports and response-planning workflows for flood-prone communities in Bangladesh.

## Short description

FloodGuard AI explores how flood information can become more useful for community health preparedness. It combines weather context and modelled hydrological estimates with transparent planning indicators, shelter medical-readiness tools, clinic-access scenarios and consent-based community reporting. English and Bangla interfaces make the prototype easier to explore. It is a research/demo decision-support tool—not an official warning service or a clinically validated forecasting system.

## Inspiration

In north-eastern Bangladesh, the Sylhet–Sunamganj haor basin faces seasonal flood risk linked to heavy upstream rainfall and connected river systems. Flooding can damage homes and crops, interrupt education and transport, contaminate water sources and make health facilities harder to reach. The project's starting question was: **How might local people and responders connect flood context with practical health-preparedness decisions before access becomes difficult?**

The project draws on published accounts of the June 2022 floods. The India Meteorological Department recorded 972 mm of rainfall at Cherrapunji in 24 hours during June 2022. The IFRC reported widespread inundation in the north-east, including around 94% of Sunamganj town and 84% of Sylhet district submerged, and estimated 7.2 million people affected across nine districts. These historical figures describe the scale of the problem; they do not validate FloodGuard AI's current models.

## What it does

1. **Flood context dashboard:** displays precipitation context from Open-Meteo when available, plus GloFAS modelled discharge estimates with source and freshness cues.
2. **Flood Health Watch:** turns configurable assumptions into hand-weighted preparedness indicators for water-related illness and snakebite response. These are planning heuristics, not clinical outbreak predictions.
3. **Shelter readiness and preposition planning:** demonstrates how teams could review sample ORS, clean-water and first-aid stock levels and explore illustrative supply gaps.
4. **Scenario tools:** includes rainfall simulation, statistical trend extrapolation, a historical rainfall archive where available and a BasinTwin graph with explicitly assumed connections.
5. **RouteSafe and impact views:** explores illustrative clinic-access, shelter and community impact scenarios; these are not verified live road or evacuation instructions.
6. **Community Ground-Truth:** allows users to submit flood-depth and road-condition observations with consent. Reports remain unverified until an authorized administrator reviews them; reporter contact numbers are not exposed in public listings.
7. **Operations Center:** includes SQLite-backed check-ins, alert rules, event streaming, responder workflows, a situation-report generator, audit records and an SMS outbox. Actual SMS delivery requires a separately configured gateway adapter.
8. **Bilingual and resilient interface:** English and Bangla UI, fixed bilingual preparedness guidance, PWA install support and cached app-shell access for previously loaded screens.

## How we built it

- **Frontend:** React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Recharts, Leaflet and Lucide icons.
- **Backend:** Express.js API for weather/hydrology proxying, Gemini calls, validation, moderation, alert publishing and operations endpoints.
- **Persistence:** SQLite for operations history, check-ins, subscriptions, rules and audit events; JSON persistence for selected community reports and public alerts.
- **AI:** Optional Google Gemini integration for selected natural-language chat and prediction explanations. Numerical indicators are calculated by deterministic application logic. Safety-critical health guidance is fixed bilingual content, not generated by Gemini.
- **Resilience:** service-worker app-shell caching and non-LLM fallbacks for supported features.

## What makes the approach different

Many flood dashboards focus on water levels alone. FloodGuard AI explores a connected workflow from flood context to health-preparedness indicators, shelter supplies, clinic access and community reports. It also makes data provenance visible so that a demonstration value is not mistaken for a verified observation.

## Challenges we worked through

- **Different data types:** rainfall, gridded hydrological model output, simulated station benchmarks and community observations have different meanings and uncertainty. The UI must not merge them into one false claim of certainty.
- **Flood-to-health translation:** the prototype uses transparent, adjustable planning heuristics rather than claiming clinical validity where no surveillance model has been established.
- **AI safety:** a language model can help explain information, but it should not invent measurements or rewrite critical health instructions.
- **Weak connectivity:** communities may have limited connectivity during a flood, so the app includes app-shell caching and fallback behaviour while clearly showing which live features need a connection.

## Accomplishments

- Integrated 20 feature views into one full-stack application.
- Built an operations workflow with SQLite persistence, alert rules, event streaming, check-ins, moderation and audit records.

- Added automated tests for risk calculations, validation, persistence, admin controls and operations workflows.

## What we learned

A useful disaster-preparedness tool must do more than display a risk score. It should explain where information came from, show what remains uncertain, connect the information to practical preparation and allow communities to contribute observations without exposing their personal details. AI is most useful here as an interface and explanation layer—not as a replacement for trusted measurements, official warnings or professional judgement.

## What's next

- Validate user needs through documented interviews with flood-affected residents and local responders.
- Integrate trusted, locally relevant river-gauge and official-warning feeds after access and licensing are confirmed.
- Compare model outputs with historical observations and publish evaluation metrics with uncertainty.
- Run supervised usability tests with residents, educators and community organisations.
- Pilot shelter readiness and low-bandwidth information flows with appropriate local partners.

## Limitations and safety

This version is a research/demo prototype. Open-Meteo precipitation may be live when reachable; GloFAS values are grid-cell model estimates. The app has no live BWDB river-stage sensor adapter. Station levels, selected shelter inventory, route conditions, satellite content and evidence-ledger examples are simulated or reference data. The health indicators are hand-weighted and not clinically validated. Statistical trend output is not a calibrated hydrodynamic forecast. Community reports remain unverified until moderated. The SMS outbox does not send messages unless a compatible gateway adapter is configured. FloodGuard AI does not replace official FFWC/BWDB/DDM warnings, local emergency services or licensed medical advice.
