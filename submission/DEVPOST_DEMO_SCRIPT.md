# FloodGuard AI — 3-Minute Devpost Demo Script

## Recording rules

- Record the actual running full-stack app, not screenshots alone.
- Use a clean browser window at 1080p; hide `.env`, admin PINs, terminal secrets and personal data.
- Do not present simulated alerts, river stages, shelter stock, satellite examples or road conditions as live.
- If live Open-Meteo data is unavailable, say so and show the fallback label rather than improvising.
- Use a calm voice, legible cursor movements and short pauses between modules.

## 0:00–0:25 — The problem

**On screen:** Landing page and cause-to-effect chain.

**Say:**

"Floods are not only a water problem. In north-eastern Bangladesh, flooding can contaminate water, interrupt transport and make health services harder to reach. I built FloodGuard AI to explore how flood information can support practical community health preparedness."

## 0:25–0:55 — The connected dashboard

**On screen:** Dashboard; open data-health/provenance detail.

**Say:**

"The dashboard brings together weather context and modelled river-discharge estimates when those sources are available. We label what is live, modelled, derived or simulated. For example, GloFAS is a gridded model estimate—not a local river-gauge reading—and our station benchmarks are demo/reference inputs."

## 0:55–1:25 — Flood-to-health preparedness

**On screen:** Health Watch and Preposition Planner.

**Say:**

"Flood Health Watch turns transparent assumptions into preparedness indicators. The shelter planner demonstrates how responders could review sample ORS, clean-water and first-aid needs. These are planning tools, not clinically validated disease predictions or real shelter inventories."

## 1:25–1:55 — Scenario and geographic tools

**On screen:** Simulator, Map, RouteSafe or BasinTwin.

**Say:**

"The simulator lets users explore hypothetical rainfall scenarios. The map and RouteSafe views help explain how location and clinic access might matter. These route and basin layers are illustrative; they are not verified safe evacuation instructions or a calibrated hydraulic model."

## 1:55–2:25 — Community and operations

**On screen:** Community Reports, then Operations Center. Use demo data only.

**Say:**

"People can contribute observations with explicit consent. Reports remain pending until an authorized administrator reviews them, and contact details are not included in public listings. The operations center demonstrates check-ins, alert rules, responder workflows, event updates and a queued notification outbox."

## 2:25–2:45 — AI and bilingual resilience

**On screen:** Assistant; toggle English/Bangla; show fixed guidance.

**Say:**

"Gemini supports selected natural-language explanations when configured. It does not generate our safety-critical health instructions or source the measurements. The interface is bilingual, and the PWA caches the app shell so previously loaded screens can remain accessible on weak connections."

## 2:45–3:00 — Close

**On screen:** Return to hero and show project name.

**Say:**

"FloodGuard AI is a prototype, not an official warning system. Our next step is real community validation and integration of trusted local data. Our goal is simple: help communities turn information into better preparation."
